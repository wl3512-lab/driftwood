Map redesign: three directions

Same functions as today: five levels (done, next, locked), stars, Watch intro, Field guide n/5, your finished garden, your garden's name. Shown mid-game (Levels 1–2 done, Level 3 next). Laptop 1512×830 and phone 844×390, side by side, then the splash and ending in the same style.

Screens that share the bland look

  • Map: flat sky band over flat green, five identical brown boxes in a row, 60% of the screen empty; on phone Level 5 drops below the fold.
  • Splash: black void with a small centred title, one bird and one button.
  • Name entry: black void, centred text block and input, nothing to look at.
  • Field guide: black CRT screen of identical dashed boxes, centred, repeats the pet name in every card.
  • Ending / finished garden: the same flat sky + flat green + brown boxes as the map, so the payoff looks like the menu.
  • Not bland: levels, almanac, reveal card, opening cutscene (these set the bar).

A · Overworld map

Your garden from above: a dirt path winds through five plots, each drawn as its own level (veg beds, greenhouse, dusk hedge, day beds, night meadow). The pet waits at its gate, stars grow as flowers on its signpost, locked plots sit under fog, and a red pin marks where you are; the path ends at your house, which opens when the garden is done.

B · Dawn to night Recommended

The cutscene's golden-hour world turned into the menu: five raised beds left to right while the sky runs from morning to moonlight, so the day itself shows your progress (Level 3 sits at dusk, Level 5 under the moon). The next bed glows with a light beam and its pet bounces; locked beds are silhouettes.

C · Seed tin

Levels are seed packets standing in a wooden tin, the same packets as the almanac shelf. The next packet is lifted, and the big window on the left shows its plot at full size with the pet, stars and a Play button, like a console's world select.
